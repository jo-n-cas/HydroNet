Pollution pressures in the Amstel, Gooi en Vecht area

This map shows industrial sites, wastewater treatment plants, waste and energy sites, and marinas, harbours and boatyards inside the AGV water board area, taken from OpenStreetMap and drawn over the OpenStreetMap base map. Each category is linked to the KRW (Water Framework Directive) substances it is likely to release. These links are based on typical sector emissions. They are not measurements and not permit data, so treat each point as a lead to check, not as a confirmed source.

Read this before you use the map

  • OpenStreetMap is not a permit register. Small companies are often missing, and many sites are mapped only as an industrial area without a name or activity.
  • Most companies discharge into the sewer, not directly into surface water. Their pollutants then show up at the outfall of a wastewater treatment plant or a sewer overflow, not next to the factory.
  • Some sites were sorted into a category by keywords in their name. A few may be in the wrong category; click a point and check the OpenStreetMap entry.
  • Parts of Amsterdam, including the port area, drain to the IJ and the Noordzeekanaal. Those are national waters managed by Rijkswaterstaat, not AGV water bodies. Check where a site actually drains before linking it to an AGV monitoring station.
  • Tributyltin is banned on ships since 2008 and cybutryne is no longer approved as a biocide in the EU. Near marinas, expect legacy residues in sediment rather than new inputs.
  • The substance lists are my own reasoning from typical sector emissions, not from your literature folder. Check them against the Emissieregistratie and AGV's own monitoring before you use them in the HydroNet analysis. The EU priority substance list is being revised (a PFAS sum and bisphenol A among the additions); check which version applies to your data years.
